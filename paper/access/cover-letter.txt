Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled “Beyond First-Order Drift: Practical WSPR-2 Decoding Robustness under Nonstationary Frequency Instability” for consideration as a Regular Manuscript in IEEE Access.

This work presents an end-to-end computational characterization of WSPR-2 decoding robustness using the unmodified production WSJT-X 3.0.2 wsprd decoder. The study distinguishes frequency evolution that lies within the decoder’s constant-plus-first-order drift model from beyond-model residual frequency dynamics, and quantifies their effects on decode probability near the weak-signal operating threshold.

The manuscript includes validation of an independent WSPR-2 waveform implementation against the official 162-symbol reference, a calibrated AWGN baseline, SNR-dependent first-order drift boundaries, controlled beyond-first-order residual experiments, matched factorial analysis, synthetic thermal and stochastic frequency-instability models, Allan-deviation-parameterized oscillator-noise sweeps, and an auxiliary eight-message generalization check. The analysis also evaluates compact residual-severity descriptors and shows that no single tested scalar descriptor generalizes uniformly across all tested trajectory families.

I believe the manuscript is relevant to IEEE Access because it connects weak-signal communication, practical decoder behavior, signal processing, oscillator-frequency stability, and reproducible computational experimentation. The work is intentionally scoped as an implementation-specific decoder-characterization study rather than a decoder-independent WSPR limit. No RF hardware, measured oscillator trace, or field-propagation measurement is claimed.
